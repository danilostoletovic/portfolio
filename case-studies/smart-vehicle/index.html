CASE STUDY 01 / EDGE AI & ROBOTICS

Smart Vehicle. From the camera to the controls.

A Raspberry Pi vehicle brings together computer vision, motor control, networking, and a native Android interface. The engineering challenge is keeping those pieces useful when they are all running at once.

  • Educational & research platform
  • Python · Kotlin · Docker
  • 4th place + Special Award

The project

Smart Vehicle, originally Pametno Vozilo, grew from hands-on robotics work at ETŠ Nikola Tesla in Niš. I created the platform to connect what happens in a camera frame with software that can control a physical vehicle.

The project combines Raspberry Pi 5 edge computing with a Kotlin / Jetpack Compose Android control station. The code is open source under the MIT license. Predrag Šubarević mentors the project, with thanks to former mentor Dejan Batanjac.

The constraint: vision cannot own the whole system

The first prototype coupled vision and motor control. When inference became busy, control had to wait. That made separation of responsibilities a practical requirement.

Camera capture, model inference, video delivery, and motor commands have different jobs. They need to cooperate without making the operator wait for every frame to finish processing.

The key decision: keep camera processing, inference, and control separate with multithreaded pipelines and Docker services.

What I built

  1. Pi Camera v2 → capture
  2. Raspberry Pi 5 → YOLO
  3. Android → view & control

On the vehicle

A Raspberry Pi 5 with 8GB RAM runs Python services for GPIO peripherals, YOLO object detection, and MJPEG video streaming. Four DC geared motors with Mecanum wheels provide movement in multiple directions, driven through L298N H-bridge motor drivers. An XL4015 converter supplies regulated power.

In the operator’s hand

The native Android client brings live video, drive commands, and telemetry into one control station. YOLO detection happens on the Pi. Google ML Kit OCR runs in the Android client to extract text from the video.

When connectivity changes

Smart Network monitors the connection and brings up a local fallback hotspot when external Wi-Fi drops. Model Pipeline provides optimization, quantization, and export workflows for TFLite and ONNX models.

What the project demonstrates

The platform earned 4th place and a Special Award at the 10th Galaksija Cup. Its public evidence includes the vehicle, Android interface, hardware diagram, and open-source implementation.

The lesson is about system boundaries: separate the expensive work, make control usable, and plan for a dropped connection. Those same decisions matter in apps and connected services.

Teleoperation and on-device vision are the foundation. LiDAR and SLAM, accessible programming tools, and clearer replication guides are future directions, not shipped capabilities.
